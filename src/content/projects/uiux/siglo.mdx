<Section order={2} title="The Influence of Influencers">
    <SectionContent>
        Regardless of who we are - rich or poor, laborer or high-status
        individual - we are all **human beings entitled to human rights**. The
        difference between an influential person and others is their ability to
        **create influence**, **initiate**, **spread**, **advocate**,
        **attract**, and **inspire**.
        {/* */}
        **There are many ways to become famous**, but given the current state of
        social media platforms, many people **become famous by conveying harmful
        and shocking titles and contents**, and naturally, this **attracts a great deal of
        public attention**.
        {/* */}
        We cannot simply blame those who **passively consume** this **content
        where the distinction between good and bad matters less than the level
        of engagement it generates**, as platform **algorithms** ensure that
        **highly interactive posts reach a wider audience**.
        {/* */}
        > ##### The Transparent Solution
        >
        > **Let users choose the content creators they truly want to follow.**
        >
        > Create an **invisible pressure of identity** where content creators are **held accountable for their** **past**, **present**, **and future actions**.
        >
        > Followers should also face similar pressure, so they **pay more attention to their** **words**, **interactions**, **respect for others**, **and accountability for themselves**.
        {/* */}
        When each individual becomes **aware of their true existence**, rather than being just an anonymous person behind a screen, they naturally **become more self-aware**.
    </SectionContent>
</Section>

<SectionHeading title="Challenges & Limitations" />

<Section order={1}>
    <SectionTitle title="Figma is not that good at prototyping" />
    <SectionContent>
        While Figma allows us to divide files into multiple pages, it **does not support linking prototypes across different pages**. As a result, we had to place all **550+ screens on a single, massive canvas** to create a complete user flow. This made the file extremely heavy and the prototype connections look like a giant spider web.
    </SectionContent>
    <MediaFrame spaceAround={false}>
        <Image
            src={`${basePath}/171.jpg`}
            alt="Spider Web"
            dim
            rounded
        />
    </MediaFrame>
    <SectionContent>
        Someone raised this issue **five years ago** - according to a <Link href="https://forum.figma.com/suggest-a-feature-11/create-one-prototype-across-the-pages-in-a-file-4463" openInNewTab showExternalIcon>post on the Figma Forum</Link>.
    </SectionContent>
    <MediaFrame spaceAround={false}>
        <Image
            src={`${basePath}/172.jpg`}
            alt="Create one prototype across the pages in a file | Figma Forum"
            dim
            rounded
        />
    </MediaFrame>
    <SectionContent>
        > ##### Exploring Advanced Alternatives
        > 
        > For future massive projects, I realized relying solely on Figma's native prototyping is not optimal. I am planning to explore professional interactive tools like **ProtoPie**. 
        >
        > Tools like this allow designers to manage screens by "Scenes" and build logic-based interactions without canvas clutter. Best of all, they offer a **seamless handoff via Figma plugins** (importing designs directly without losing layers). Mastering these advanced solutions will definitely be my next step to optimize the prototyping workflow.
    </SectionContent>
</Section>

<Section order={2}>
    <SectionTitle title="Handling feedback from the council" />
    <SectionContent>
        Defending the project across **4 milestone presentations (0%, 30%, 70%,
        100%)** acted as real-world stakeholder reviews.
        {/* */}
        > There were times when the council pointed out **flaws in the user flow**, or **identity verification processes** (SIL-1 & SIL-2) **were not needed and time consuming**.
        {/* */}
        The Siglo project started in Q1 2025, and as of Q3 2026, **Vietnam is
        gradually pushing and requiring social media platforms to verify user
        identities** to minimize online fraud.
        {/* */}
        > I needed to **filter the feedback**, deciding which to adopt and which to challenge. I chose to **retain the identity verification processes** - a feature that **makes Siglo unique**.
        {/* */}
        I learned not to be emotionally attached to my initial designs.
        {/* */}
        > **Accepting feedback, pivoting quickly, and defending UX decisions with
        data and psychology** was the most valuable soft skill I gained.
    </SectionContent>
</Section>
